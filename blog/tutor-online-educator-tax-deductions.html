Tutor and Online Educator Tax Deductions

What independent tutors and course creators can write off, from teaching materials to platform fees.

TT Tax Tools Editorial Team · December 21, 2026 · 7 min read · 582 words
Share:

Whether you tutor students one-on-one, teach through an online marketplace, or sell a self-paced course, independent educators have a distinct deduction profile built around materials, platform fees, and the technology that makes remote teaching possible.

Textbooks, workbooks, practice tests, printed worksheets, and any curriculum you purchase or license to teach from are fully deductible. If you develop your own materials to sell as part of a course, the software or design tools used to build them (worksheet templates, video editing software, slide design tools) are deductible as well.

Platform and marketplace fees

If you tutor through an online marketplace, the platform's commission or listing fees are deductible business expenses — treat them the same way an Etsy seller treats marketplace fees. If you run your own course platform instead, hosting fees, payment processing fees, and email marketing software used to reach students are all deductible.

Larger equipment purchases can often be expensed in full the year you buy them — see our Section 179 vs. bonus depreciation guide for how that works.

Home office for planning and recording

A dedicated space used regularly and exclusively for lesson planning, grading, or recording course content can qualify for the home office deduction. Full rules — including the two calculation methods — are in our home office deduction guide.

Certifications and subject-matter credentials

  • Subject-specific certifications (TEFL/TESOL for language tutors, specialized test-prep certifications).
  • Background check fees required by tutoring platforms.
  • Continuing education that maintains or sharpens skills in your existing subject area.
Related read: If your tutoring business also includes selling downloadable worksheets or templates, see our Etsy Seller Tax Deductions guide for how digital product sales are treated.

Travel for in-person tutoring

If you tutor students in their homes, libraries, or a shared learning center, mileage between sessions is deductible using the standard mileage rate or actual expenses. Commuting from your own home to your very first appointment of the day is not deductible — only travel between job locations during the day. See our mileage rate guide for the exact rule.

Self-employment tax

Once tutoring or course income turns a consistent profit, it's subject to self-employment tax in addition to income tax, and quarterly estimated payments are usually required since no one withholds tax from your invoices. See our self-employment tax guide and quarterly estimated taxes guide.

Frequently asked questions

Can I deduct the cost of taking a course myself to learn a new subject to teach?

Education that maintains or improves skills in your existing tutoring subject is generally deductible. Coursework that qualifies you to teach an entirely new subject you don't currently teach may be treated differently.

Do I need to charge sales tax on a self-paced course I sell?

This depends on your state and whether digital educational products are taxable there — rules vary significantly, so check your state's specific guidance or consult a tax professional.

What if I tutor as a side gig alongside a full-time W-2 job?

The tutoring income is still self-employment income subject to its own tax rules and deductions, reported separately from your W-2 wages, even though you file both on the same personal tax return.
