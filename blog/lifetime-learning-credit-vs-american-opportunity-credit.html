Lifetime Learning Credit vs. American Opportunity Credit: Which Education Credit Fits You

Two education tax credits, two very different rulebooks. Here's how freelancers investing in new skills should choose.

TT Tax Tools Editorial Team · September 3, 2026 · 7 min read · 484 words
Share:

Investing in new skills — a certification, a degree program, a professional course — can come with a tax credit attached, but the two major education credits work very differently, and you can only claim one per student per year.

Side-by-side comparison

FeatureAmerican Opportunity CreditLifetime Learning Credit
Eligible programsDegree-seeking, first four years of postsecondary educationAny postsecondary course, including single classes and grad school
Enrollment requirementAt least half-time in a degree programNo minimum enrollment level
Refundable portionPartially refundableNonrefundable
Per-year limitAvailable for 4 tax years per studentUnlimited number of years

Why freelancers often lean toward the Lifetime Learning Credit

Freelancers and self-employed professionals frequently take single courses, certifications, or skill-building classes rather than pursuing a full degree — a coding bootcamp, a design certification, a real estate continuing-education course. The Lifetime Learning Credit does not require degree-seeking or half-time enrollment, which makes it the more realistic fit for most working adults investing in skills rather than a diploma.

Business deduction alternative: if the course directly maintains or improves skills required in your existing trade or business, it may instead qualify as a deductible business expense on your Schedule C rather than (or in addition to, depending on the specifics) an education credit. This is a nuanced area — compare both paths before filing, ideally with professional guidance.

Income limits apply to both

Both credits phase out above certain Modified Adjusted Gross Income thresholds, which adjust periodically. High earners in a strong freelance year may find themselves phased out of one or both credits entirely — another reason keeping AGI accurate through legitimate business deductions matters even beyond your Schedule C.

What expenses actually qualify

  • Tuition and mandatory enrollment fees
  • Required course materials, in some cases, depending on the credit and institution
  • Generally excludes room, board, transportation, and insurance

You cannot double-dip

You cannot claim both credits for the same student in the same tax year, and you cannot claim a credit for expenses paid with certain tax-free educational assistance, such as scholarships already excluded from income. Keep your 1098-T and payment records organized to substantiate whichever credit you claim.

Frequently asked questions

Can I claim an education credit for my own professional development as a freelancer?

Yes, if the coursework is at a qualifying postsecondary institution and you meet the income requirements, the Lifetime Learning Credit is commonly available for professional development courses.

Is a coding bootcamp eligible?

It depends on whether the bootcamp is offered through or in partnership with an eligible postsecondary institution — not all standalone bootcamps qualify, so verify the institution's eligibility before assuming a credit applies.

Can I claim this credit and also deduct the course as a business expense?

Generally you cannot claim a credit and a business deduction for the exact same dollar of expense — coordinate the two carefully, and consider professional guidance if the amounts are significant.
